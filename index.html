Notandia adds configurable publisher context and formal post-publication signals to scholarly literature. It exposes evidence, user-selected treatment, chronology, provenance, and coverage instead of assigning an opaque quality score.

MDPI and Frontiers are built-in, independently deactivable, and highlighted by default. Each publisher can instead use context-only, badge, dim, hide, or a custom color.

Formal article events

DOI checks expose retractions, concerns, corrections, reinstatements, withdrawals, removals, and duplicate-publication findings with provenance and chronology.

Locally configurable

Custom publishers

Users can add validated profiles using publisher domains and DOI prefixes. Profiles are declarative data and cannot contain executable scripts or page selectors.

Publisher context

A watchlist is a personal display rule, not a universal verdict.

Notandia identifies evidence that an article, reference, or search result belongs to a configured publisher. The user decides whether to show context, add a badge, highlight, dim, hide, or disable that publisher entirely.

Interpretation: a publisher match does not mean that every article from that publisher is unreliable. Publisher identity and formal work-level events are displayed independently.

Work-level taxonomy

Color supports the meaning; it never replaces the label.

Product principles

Evidence before verdicts.

Scope is explicit

Publisher identity, personal display rules, and formal article events remain separate.

Coverage is visible

Checked, unresolved, failed, and deferred identifiers are reported instead of silently treated as clear.

Control is local

Publisher profiles and actions are stored and evaluated in the browser. Network-assisted DOI checks remain independently optional.

Trust boundaries

Open source. No product analytics. No hidden score.
